How this report is built

A script scans the same 19 outlets on a timer, keeping anything published in the last 36 hours. Headlines about the same event are clustered together; a story's rank is how many of the 19 outlets carried that event, not how recent or prominent any single article was.

For each story, the summary paragraph is drawn from whichever panel outlet(s) are covering that specific story most closely — the most real articles in the cluster, not a fixed subset. Every outlet in the 19-outlet panel is eligible; which outlet that turns out to be varies story by story, and is marked on each card. Any source material pulled in this way is normalized to a neutral, matter-of-fact tone regardless of how the original outlet framed it. A story can still rank highly with only thin coverage from any single outlet; it just won't have much summary, only a coverage count.

Before anything is ranked, an AI pass audits each cluster so that, say, a company's stock move and an unrelated lawsuit against that same company don't get lumped into one inflated "story." After a summary is written, it's checked back against the source articles for claims that aren't actually in them (shown below as entailment), and separately screened for AI-writing artifacts like a sentence trailing off mid-attribution (snippet form). Items tagged anything other than clean / ok passed publication anyway but are flagged so you can judge the summary yourself rather than take it on faith.

Google News is used only as a delivery mechanism for outlets that block direct scraping — every article is still credited to the publisher that actually wrote it, never to "Google News" as if it were a source.
